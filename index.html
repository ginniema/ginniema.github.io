    About

    I'm a fourth year PhD student in the Department of Statistics at Stanford University, advised by Emmanuel Candès. I am gratefully supported by a Stanford HAI Data Science Scholarship.

    My research focuses on statistical theory and methods for AI-assisted inference. I study how to make reliable inferences when labels are scarce but model predictions are abundant, when pretrained models improve decision-making, and how to evaluate language models through unsupervised ensembling of LLM judges. I am also interested in understanding how imputation and synthetic data affect downstream inferences and decisions, and in developing methods that preserve them. My work is motivated by applications in social and biomedical sciences.

    Before Stanford, I received an A.B. in Mathematics and a concurrent A.M. in Statistics from Harvard University, where I was advised by Lucas Janson for my senior thesis on empirical Bayes. In summer 2026, I was a quantitative research intern on the Alternative Data Team at Citadel Securities.
